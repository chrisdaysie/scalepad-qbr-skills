# QBR Story

Create a decision-oriented executive story from completed evidence. Curate; do not dump every metric or repeat the raw reviews.

## Inputs

Require one exact client, review period, planning horizon, audience or client tier when known, and evidence slices with stable IDs or links. Useful slices include client context, roadmap, service, security, technology, commercial, and prior QBR decisions.

If a material claim lacks evidence, mark it as unknown or return it as a coverage gap. Do not perform broad new discovery inside this skill. Load a relevant available specialist only when one focused gap blocks the story.

## Build the narrative

Use this arc:

1. **Where we were:** the client's priorities and prior decisions.
2. **What changed:** material work, events, and decisions during the period.
3. **Where we are:** current outcomes, risk, and confidence.
4. **What is next:** the roadmap and immediate sequence.
5. **What needs a decision:** preferably two or three consequential asks or acknowledged deferrals.

Lead with client business outcomes. Use service, security, technology, and commercial metrics only when they prove progress, exposure, timing, or a decision. Translate technical details into business language without losing the supporting evidence pointer.

Scale the depth to the relationship. A foundational client needs a short operationally grounded story; a strategic client may need richer goals, risk, investment, and multi-quarter sequencing. Do not add sections merely to look comprehensive.

## Draft the deliverable shape

Prefer one curated draft with:

- executive overview;
- progress against client goals;
- current roadmap and dependencies;
- material service, security, technology, and commercial signals;
- budget or investment context when authoritative;
- two or three decision items;
- owned next steps;
- an evidence appendix or drill-down references.

Keep detailed ticket lists, asset tables, control evidence, and raw assessment data out of the executive body unless the audience requests them.

Every claim must be one of observation, inference, recommendation, or unknown. Preserve proposal versus commitment and accepted risk versus unaddressed risk.

## Return

Produce:

### Executive summary

A concise narrative suitable for a draft deliverable.

### Meeting flow

An agenda ordered around outcomes and decisions rather than product modules.

### Decision cards

For each decision: ask, recommendation, business rationale, trade-off, owner, decision window, and evidence.

### Roadmap view

Goal-linked initiatives by quarter or sequence, including dependencies and proposed versus approved state.

### Follow-through

Only confirmed actions with owners and dates when known; keep undecided recommendations separate.

### Coverage notes

Contradictions, missing evidence, confidence, and material details relegated to drill-down.

This output is a draft. Do not publish, share, send, or create a deliverable record.
